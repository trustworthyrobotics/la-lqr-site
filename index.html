Activation Steering of Video Generation Models via Reduced-Order Linear Optimal Control

Jihoon Hong  ·  Alice Chan  ·  Qiyue Dai  ·  Julian Skifstad  ·  Glen Chou

Trustworthy Robotics Lab  ·  Georgia Institute of Technology

Paper Code
Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Code and prompts are in the la-lqr repository.
TL;DR

Text-to-video models trained on web data will happily render nudity, gore or hate when asked. LA-LQR (Latent Activation Linear-Quadratic Regulator) treats video generation as a dynamical system and computes closed-loop steering signals for the text-embedding activations of a diffusion transformer: it projects activations onto a low-dimensional subspace learned from contrastive prompt pairs, estimates local linear dynamics there, and solves a latent LQR problem so that every layer and timestep nudges the activation toward a safe feature setpoint, only as much as needed. No finetuning, no weight edits. On T2VSafetyBench (Wan2.1-14B) and SafeSora (HunyuanVideo-1.5) it cuts the violation rate from 36.9% to 14.6% and from 31.5% to 12.2% while keeping VBench quality and prompt fidelity.

Abstract

Text-to-video (T2V) models trained on large-scale web data can generate undesired content, motivating interventions that reduce harmful outputs without sacrificing visual quality. Activation steering offers an attractive mechanistic alternative to finetuning and prompt filtering, but existing T2V steering methods remain limited, typically applying coarse non-anticipative interventions that can lead to oversteering and content degradation. To close this gap, we propose Latent Activation Linear-Quadratic Regulator (LA-LQR), a reduced-order optimal control framework for minimally invasive T2V steering. LA-LQR formulates T2V inference as a dynamical system and computes closed-loop feedback interventions that steer activations toward desired feature setpoints while penalizing unnecessary perturbations.

To make optimal control feasible for high-dimensional video activations, we project activations onto a low-dimensional, task-relevant subspace derived from contrastive prompt pairs, estimate local linear dynamics in this latent space, and solve a latent LQR problem to obtain timestep- and layer-specific steering signals. We provide theoretical bounds relating latent setpoint tracking to raw activation-space feature control, and empirically validate the fidelity of the reduced latent dynamics. On concept steering and video safety benchmarks, LA-LQR reduces unsafe generations relative to baselines, while preserving prompt fidelity and visual quality.

Method

Closed-loop, reduced-order control of a diffusion transformer

Given N prompt pairs that differ only in the target feature (for example the same scene with and without nudity), we collect the DiT video-token activations at every layer and denoising step and build a contrastive vector per pair. These vectors define both the feature direction and, through a streaming randomized SVD, a rank-64 latent subspace per group of layers. Control happens in that subspace.

Open-loop · prior work

Contrastive addition / filtering

Add a fixed steering vector with a hand-tuned strength at every step, or remove toxic directions from the text embedding before denoising. Neither looks at the activation the model actually produced, so interventions over- or under-steer: too weak and the harmful concept survives, too strong and the video falls apart (artifacts, lost scene content).

Closed-loop · ours

LA-LQR

Treat the chain of transformer blocks and sampler steps as a nonlinear time-varying system, linearize it in the latent subspace with Jacobian-vector products, and solve a Riccati recursion once. Online, each block measures how far the realized latent activation is from the safe setpoint and applies the minimum-norm correction to the text embeddings.

Results

Fewer violations, same video quality

Violation rate is judged by GPT-4o on sampled frames following the T2VSafetyBench protocol (lower is better); quality is measured with VBench and prompt fidelity with CAPS, the cosine similarity between GPT-4o descriptions of the steered and unsteered video. Baselines: SAFREE (training-free text-embedding filtering), Video Unlearning (low-rank refusal vector) and text-embedding interpolation steering.
